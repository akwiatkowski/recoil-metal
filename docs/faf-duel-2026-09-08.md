## How kills happen

First exchange: both scouts fire at 01:53.3; their first logged damage is at 01:55.5.
Projectile launch and damage are separate events. At 02:18.7 UEF assault bot `23.1`
(`UEL0106`) kills Aeon scout `15.1` (`UAL0101`). That 20-HP scout had taken four
4-damage hits from UEF scouts `14.1` and `19.1`; the bot supplies the final 7-damage
hit. Kill credit belongs to the last hitter, not every contributing attacker.

| Killer | Victim | Last-hit kills |
|---|---|---:|
| UEF Striker `UEL0201` | Aeon Aurora `UAL0201` | 289 |
| Aeon Aurora `UAL0201` | UEF Striker `UEL0201` | 224 |
| UEF Striker | Aeon land scout | 116 |
| Aeon Shimmer bomber `UAA0103` | UEF Striker | 107 |
| Aeon Aurora | UEF land scout | 102 |
| UEF Scorcher bomber `UEA0103` | Aeon Aurora | 54 |

All 55 same-army last hits match a killer's death tick and subsequent death-explosion
damage. Example: at 20:35.5 an enemy kills UEF bomber `12.3` at (5232,2725); its
100-damage explosion hits many co-located aircraft, killing scouts `71.2` and `76.3`
on the next tick (20:35.6). Fifty-three friendly deaths have a final 100-damage hit;
two generators die to an allied energy-storage explosion for 2,000 damage each.

The current sim resolves death blasts after retiring that tick's dead units, so blast
victims are retired on the next tick (`src/core/sim/Skirmish.cpp`, death pass). Thus a
fatal damage timestamp can precede the death notification by 0.1 s. UEF/Aeon bomber
blueprints author `DeathImpact`, 100 damage, and `DamageFriendly = true`. The evidence
supports death-blast chains, not intentional allied targeting. See `kills.csv` for
victim/killer identities, final damage time/value, prior damage-event count and attackers.
Damage values in the existing text logger are rounded to whole HP and are post-mitigation
incoming damage before overkill clipping; they cannot reconstruct exact health curves.

## Readiness gaps exposed

- No victory after one simulated hour; both commanders finish at full current maximum HP.
  UEF's commander has 18 logged damage events; Aeon's has 2,936. Neither is immune.
- 90 naval units completed (29 UEF frigates; 60 Aeon AA boats and one frigate), but naval
  units fired only one shot and killed nothing. Production alone is not useful combat.
- 4,394 of 6,352 logged attack dispatches send one unit. These are repeated order
  dispatches, not unique battles or independently formed platoons. They support a
  concern about piecemeal pressure, not a proved sole cause of stalemate.
- Six decision-pass watchdog failures: army 0 around 46:16 and 46:19; army 1 around
  55:04, 55:22, 55:25 and 59:05. The report also lists four condition errors caused by
  instruction-budget exhaustion (2 + 1 + 1). Keep decision and condition totals separate.
- Sandbox: 107 modules executed, nine missing/not vendored, zero failed modules and zero
  coroutine errors. The long run exposes failures that shorter sanity checks missed.

Next: profile the late instruction overruns and inspect attack-target/formation decisions,
naval routing/targets and aircraft clustering. These observations do not justify disabling
reclaim or changing balance constants without a focused experiment.

## Follow-up: scout churn and missing factory upgrades

Read-only investigation of the same run and adapter, September 8. No balance or
simulation changes were made and no additional match was launched.

### Scouts were repeatedly replaced

Reconstructing living scouts from completion/death records gives UEF 133 built,
132 killed, peak four alive and median lifetime 58.1 seconds; Aeon 147 built,
144 killed, peak five alive and median lifetime 49.8 seconds. Enemy tanks killed
102 UEF scouts and 116 Aeon scouts.

`AIIntelBuilders.lua:136` replenishes land scouts whenever fewer than two live
scouts remain, at priority 875, above tanks at 825. The separate ratio builder
at line 167 targets 10% of mobile land non-engineers. Snapshot counts exclude
dead units. These are lifetime production totals, not a large standing scout force.

`T1LandScoutForm` requests `ScoutingAI` (`LandPlatoonTemplates.lua:105`), but our
adapter turns every non-upgrade platoon into a generic attack decision
(`FafOpponent.cpp:935`) and moves it toward the nearest enemy commander (:1902).
FAF's scouting plan instead visits scouting interest areas with threat-aware
paths (`platoon.lua:1171`). Also, `brain.LowEnergyMode` is initialized false
(:455) and never refreshed, so the initial scout builder's low-power gate never
suppresses production. Logs do not identify which scout builder selected each
product; cached conditions and the ratio builder could both explain modest
overshoot above two. Lowering the scout ratio would not address the missing plan.

### Factory progression has three separate blockers

The adapter hardcodes `NormalMain` (`FafOpponent.cpp:1272`), whose authored
personality is `easy` and whose groups are `T1SlowUpgradeBuilders` and
`T2SlowUpgradeBuilders`. It does not choose a balanced/rush personality for this map.
The active requirements are in `AIEconomyUpgradeBuilders.lua:748` onward.
Economy APIs use per-tick income; the following table converts to per second.

| Upgrade | Required mass/s | Required energy/s | Other relevant requirements |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| T1 land to T2 | 50 | 500 | At least one T2/T3 extractor |
| T1 air to T2 | 50 | 400 | More than one factory overall |
| T2 land/air to T3 | 90 | 1,000 | T3 extractor and at least four T2 engineers |

All also require mass income/requested demand >=0.9 and energy >=1.2.
The adapter publishes instantaneous values into `EconomyOverTimeCurrent`, rather
than maintaining an actual historical average (`FafOpponent.cpp:730`).

1. **Land: income gate.** The 720 five-second samples per army peak at UEF
   66.5 mass/s, 425 energy/s and Aeon 68.5 mass/s, 305 energy/s. Neither sampled
   economy reaches the T2 land power threshold. T1 power builders react to
   energy trends below 15/25 energy/s (`AIEconomicBuilders.lua:2559`), rather
   than reserving power for future technology; a production-sustaining economy
   can therefore stop expanding below the slow-upgrade threshold. This policy
   mismatch is a plausible contributor, not a counterfactual experiment.
2. **Air: UEF tried, but the factory was busy.** UEF meets the air income gate
   in 99 samples and both income/efficiency gates in only two. The command log
   contains three UEB0202 upgrade requests at ticks 34420, 34430 and 34440
   (57:22–57:24), IDs 8503, 8507 and 8511, all with zero accepted units.
   Its sole air factory, 55.1, was building bomber 360.3 from tick 34340 through
   34539 (57:14–57:33.9). Upgrade selection checks `idle`, defined as not moving,
   but ignores `building` (`FafOpponent.cpp:923,1500`). The command path rejects
   a builder with active construction (`Command.cpp:2568`). Requests are not
   queued or retained until the factory is free. Additionally, production is
   selected before upgrades and upgrade selection ignores `__taken`, allowing
   the same conflict on a free factory in one decision pass. Aeon never meets
   the air income gate in the sampled economy.
3. **Navy: deliberately disabled.** `FafOpponent.cpp:563` removes both T1 and T2
   sea upgrade templates from the active groups. More resources cannot overcome
   this explicit T1-only scope restriction.

T3 is blocked upstream by the absence of T2 factories and engineers, as well as
its larger economy and T3-extractor requirements. This run does not establish
whether every later T3 adapter path works once those prerequisites exist.

The next focused change should reserve a factory for an eligible upgrade and
honor existing production before dispatching it, with a headless regression
covering the busy and same-pass cases. Then evaluate the intended AI personality
and technology power budget. Scouting needs its own movement behavior; naval
T2/T3 requires intentionally extending the supported naval slice. Do not simply
reduce scout counts or lower upgrade thresholds to conceal these adapter gaps.

## Factory upgrade fix verification

The subsequent adapter fix (ADR-112) reserves a factory before production,
retains its eligible upgrade while the current product finishes, and discards
reservations for dead/replaced or already-upgrading units. Economy thresholds
and naval scope remain unchanged.

The same offscreen 60-minute duel now records one accepted UEB0202 command at
tick 34540 (57:34), targeting factory 55.1 immediately after its bomber completes.
The T2 air factory completes at 3543.9 seconds (59:03.9), followed by UEA0203 at
3586.9 seconds (59:46.9). The original run's three rejected upgrade attempts are
replaced by a successful construction and subsequent production.

The new headless regression first failed on the busy-factory case, then passed
for retained intent across an economy drop, same-pass production exclusion,
already-upgrading units, and generation-safe reservation cleanup. Full CTest:
1,510 passed, two optional skips. The scripted golden matched all 7,000 ticks.
The offscreen duel exited successfully and wrote 36,000 hashes; its six late
decision-budget failures remain a separate known issue. This fixed duel has not
been independently repeated for a hash comparison.
